How we estimate savings, and where they can be wrong

Unused capacity is priced at AWS's published rates for your region. Savings from cheaper models, work that can wait and reused text are estimated from what you actually spend on each model, always as a range, with a simple test to confirm each one.

Discounts you've negotiated with AWS make your real costs lower than published prices, and a cheaper model needs testing on your own examples before you switch. Every finding shows its evidence so you can judge it.

Savings that don't put your product at risk

Every recommendation is checked so it won't slow your product down or make it less dependable, and every change is planned so it can be undone where possible. We flag the ones that can't, like a deleted resource or a commitment, and if a saving carries real risk, we tell you and you decide.

The scan is free. AWS charges about 1 cent for each cost report the scan reads, so one scan adds about 20 to 40 cents to your AWS bill.

Unused capacity is priced at AWS's published rates, or the rate on your bill. Savings from cheaper models, work that can wait and reused text are estimates with a range, based on what you actually spend. Discounts you've negotiated lower real costs, so we check large items against your bill.

Can you make the changes for us?

Yes. After the audit we quote each fix you want as its own project, such as budgets and alerts, moving work that can wait, or switching simple tasks to cheaper models. Your team can also do it all from the report.

It shouldn't. Guides for your engineers

Short technical answers to the questions we hear most, with sources.

When Bedrock batch inference saves money

Batch inference processes a file of requests in the background and returns results later, at 50% less than on-demand pricing for supported models. It fits work nobody waits on: nightly report summaries, bulk document tagging, evaluation runs. It doesn't fit chat or anything a user is waiting for, since results can take up to 24 hours.

Estimate the saving as spend on that model, times the share of work that can wait, times 50%.

Sources: AWS Bedrock pricing, DoiT

Prompt caching: when it pays

When many requests start with the same long text, such as system instructions, a reference document or tool definitions, Bedrock can cache that shared prefix, and cached input tokens cost about 90% less. Steady traffic benefits most, because cached prefixes expire after a few minutes.

Caching only cuts input costs, and output tokens typically cost 3 to 5 times more than input, so output-heavy apps save less. Support and minimum prefix length vary by model.

Source: DoiT

Is Bedrock provisioned throughput worth it?

Provisioned throughput reserves model capacity that bills every hour, used or not. Fine-tuned models need it, and it can beat on-demand for steady, high-volume traffic. DoiT puts the break-even at roughly 60 to 70% sustained utilization of a model unit; below that, on-demand is usually cheaper.

Reservations left running after a project ends are one of the most expensive Bedrock mistakes. Committed terms keep billing until they end, so review renewals early.

Source: DoiT

Finding idle SageMaker endpoints and GPU instances

A real-time SageMaker endpoint bills for its instances every hour, even with zero traffic, and GPU instances on EC2 do the same. Check each endpoint's invocations in CloudWatch over the last two to four weeks, and GPU utilization for GPU instances, which needs the CloudWatch agent's NVIDIA metrics.

Before deleting anything, confirm nothing calls it monthly. For occasional traffic, serverless or async endpoints that scale to zero are usually cheaper.

Sources: AWS: analyze SageMaker hosting spend, AWS: collect NVIDIA GPU metrics, AWS: scale an endpoint to zero
